title: "The rule"
description: "Two quotes, and the taker gets the worse one."
---

## Why two quotes

A pool's own reserves say what it can afford in isolation. The federation's aggregate reserves say
what the pair can afford in total. When a pool is skewed, those disagree — and the gap is exactly
the value an arbitrageur would take.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Local pool quotes">
    Constant product against this pool's own reserves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Federation quotes">
    The same maths against the sum of every member's reserves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take the worse one">
    `min` for exact input, `max` for exact output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The difference stays">
    The taker never receives the surplus. It remains in the local pool.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Worked example

Pool A holds 1,000 token0 and 1,500 token1. Pool B holds 1,000 and 500. Alone, Pool A offers far
more token1 for a new token0 trade. Together the pair is balanced at 2,000 / 2,000.

If Pool A would pay 14.8 token1 and the aggregate supports 9.9, the trader receives **9.9**. The
remaining 4.9 stays in Pool A, and both books update atomically.

## What binds, and when

The bound only bites when the local pool is the generous one, which happens when it is skewed
relative to the pair. Across 1,001 randomised configurations it never once bound on a trade that
was widening divergence.

| Skew | Value withheld |
| --- | --- |
| 1× (balanced) | 0 bps |
| 2× | 4,299 bps |
| 4× | 6,674 bps |
| 8× | 7,862 bps |

A pool in line with its pair is untouched. The mechanism engages in proportion to fragmentation.
